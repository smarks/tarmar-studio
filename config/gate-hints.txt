# The hints the passphrase gate shows a visitor who has guessed wrong
# GATE_HINT_AFTER_WRONG_GUESSES times (issues #55, #77). One is drawn at
# random for every refusal from that guess on, and for the 429 once the
# guessing budget is spent.
#
# This file is configuration, not code: Spencer edits it with no Python. One
# hint per line. Blank lines and lines starting with # are ignored.
# Apostrophes and quotation marks are allowed. The file is read once, when
# Django's settings load (tarmar_studio/settings.py, GATE_HINTS), and a
# missing or empty file stops the server at startup rather than at a
# visitor's first wrong guess. Another file can be named with the
# GATE_HINTS_FILE environment variable.
#
# The hints are meant to embarrass the asker for not knowing the lore, and to
# stay sneaky: none says the word itself.

The Doors of Durin stood for an age against orcs and worse. Against you they are not even trying.
Gandalf solved this on a cold night with wolves behind him and something hungry in the lake. You have a search engine and a chair.
Merry Brandybuck asked the one question that opens this door. He was a hobbit. He had never been further than Bree. Catch up.
This is the most quoted door in fantasy literature. The answer is written on it. In Elvish. Read it again.
Celebrimbor wrote the answer into the door in plain Elvish and assumed any passer-by could read it. He had never met you.
Everyone who has read The Fellowship of the Ring knows this word. Everyone who has only seen the film knows it too. Which leaves you.
The dwarves of Moria had words for guests who could not read the front door. None of them was friend.
Elvish for friend. That is the whole riddle. A ten-year-old reading under the covers with a flashlight got it in 1954.
