# Hook Introduction

Five ways to put an adventure in front of the characters. Mix them: the best openings use two.

## Patrons

Someone asks for help and offers payment. The question that makes the hook work is why they want outsiders.

| d6  | Why hire outsiders                     |
| --- | -------------------------------------- |
| 1   | Their own people cannot be trusted     |
| 2   | They need skills no one local has      |
| 3   | There is no time to find anyone better |
| 4   | The job must not be traced to them     |
| 5   | Everyone local has already refused     |
| 6   | They believe the characters owe them   |

## Consequences

Something the characters did, or failed to do, in an earlier session comes back. An enemy they spared, a debt they left, a door they opened.

## Places

The world itself offers the hook: a ruin seen from the road, a town in mourning, smoke where no village should be.

## Information

A rumour, a map, a letter delivered to the wrong person. Information pulls hardest when it is incomplete and someone else is also following it.

## Personal Ties

A friend or relative of one character is in trouble. Use the character's prior experience to find the tie: a former master, an old comrade, a sibling still at home.
