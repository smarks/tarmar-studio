## The World of the Corran Reach
Grounded fantasy on a frontier: small stakes that matter to the people who hold them, old ruins nobody understands, and magic that is real but rare. This is the default canon, a stand-in world that ships with the app; it is not the setting of any published campaign. Play is travel, trade, trouble between neighbours, and the occasional descent into something older than the kingdom. Reward players who ask who built a thing and why, not only what it is worth. For most people here the world runs from their village to the nearest market and no farther.

### The Lands
The known world is the wreck of an older one. The Lantern Kings, a line of sorcerer-rulers whose names survive only on boundary stones, once held every coast within a month's sail; they built the paved Barrow Roads that still carry traffic and raised barrows on every hill, then fell so completely that no one agrees how. What remains is a scatter of kingdoms, free towns and hill clans living among their ruins. Play begins in the Corran Reach, the westernmost march of the kingdom of Eskarth. Beyond it lie Eskarth itself, three weeks' ride east; the Uplands, moor and pine past the pass at Ashcombe, where hill clans owe Eskarth nothing; and Port Ilvane, a merchant city across the western sea.

### The Corran Reach (where most journeys begin)
The Corran is a short, wide river running west out of chalk hills, through a valley of farms and mills, into a tidal estuary a mile across. At its mouth stands Brinemouth, a harbour town of salt pans and herring boats that closes its harbour every night with an iron chain. Lantern Rock, a black stack at the estuary's outer edge, carries a light-tower far older than the town. On the north shore the sea took the village of the Drowned Bell three lifetimes ago; its church tower still shows at low tide. South of the estuary lies Greyfen, a salt marsh of eel-catchers and smugglers' channels. Upriver are Hollin Ford, the market town at the lowest crossing, with its tolled bridge and hereditary ford-wardens; Brannock, the margrave's castle on its crag; Ostry Abbey, a house of healers on a river island; and Wendle Mill, where the Millers' Guild grinds the valley's grain at a price everyone resents. In the Tallow Hills, chalk downland of sheep and barrows, stands the closed lead and silver mine of Harrowdeep, and at the head of the valley the fort of Ashcombe watches the pass into the Uplands with a garrison of forty that ought to be sixty.

### The Kingdom of Eskarth
A middling kingdom ruled from a court far to the east, which takes the Reach's taxes and levies and otherwise leaves the margrave to keep order as he can. Its ranks run king or queen, prince or princess, duke or duchess, margrave, count or countess, baron or baroness, knight; stewards, reeves and wardens hold offices rather than titles, and are often more powerful in practice than the lords they serve.

### The Powers
- The Margrave of the Reach governs the march for the crown from Brannock. He is old, careful and short of money; his heir is young, bold and shorter of it, and owes the Ilvane factors more than his father knows.
- The factors of Port Ilvane keep a counting house in Brinemouth, buy most of the Reach's salt before it is boiled and its wool before it is shorn, and lend to anyone who will sign.
- The healing order of Ostry Abbey keeps an infirmary open to all, a physic garden, and a library it does not lend. Its abbess is the best-read person in the Reach and the one the margrave consults unasked.
- The Millers' Guild of Eskarth owns the mills at Wendle and sets the price of grinding for the whole valley.
- The ford-wardens of Hollin Ford toll the bridge, judge market disputes, and pass the office down in their families.
- Magic is real, uncommon and distrusted. Hedge-wizards and abbey herbalists are tolerated; a caster who can bend fire or weather is watched. Most people have seen a spell once or twice and would rather not see a third.

### Peoples
Humans are nearly everyone. The hill clans of the Uplands are human too, and trade wool, ponies and amber at the border fairs when they are not raiding. Dwarves are known in the Reach as smiths and traders who come down the Barrow Roads in summer; dwarf-made tools and armour are worth twice the price of local work. Elves are rumoured more often than met. In the wild places there are wolves, boar and the great moor bears of the Uplands; in the barrows and deep ruins, the restless dead.

### History as the Educated Tell It
The Lantern Kings ruled long before Eskarth and are remembered for their roads, their barrows and their fall. The scholars of the abbeys hold that the Kings drew more power than any living mage dares, and that the ruins are what drawing it cost. After the fall came centuries of small lords and shifting borders, until Eskarth gathered the eastern valleys under one crown and pushed west to the sea. The Reach was the last land it took; the margrave's line has held it for five generations.

### GM-Only Background (never state to players; hint at most)
The Lantern Kings drew their power through fire: each kept a tower whose light was the visible end of a working that reached far below it, and the towers were raised on stone cut for the purpose. When the Kings fell the workings went out, and the ruins along every coast are where they burned out worst. Lantern Rock is one of the few towers whose stone is still warm, because something beneath it was never quite put out; the keeping family suspects this and will not say so. The hall the Harrowdeep miners broke into belongs to the same age. Leave what lies beyond its door, and what the Drowned Bell's crypt held, to the scenario tables and the Game Master; this section does not decide them. Deeper secrets than these exist and are not written here; do not invent them.
