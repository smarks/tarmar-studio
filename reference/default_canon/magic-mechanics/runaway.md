# Runaway

When magic slips from control, it feeds on the caster. The spell destabilises, and the caster's reserves drain away: first mana, then life. Each round the caster tries to regain control with an **Anchor Roll** (3d6 against Wisdom), and each round the task grows harder.

When the pool is empty the caster falls unconscious and the magic begins to consume the caster's Body. When the Body fails, the caster dies.

Another mage may try to intervene and help. If the intervention fails, both are caught in the spell.

On the battle board this default canon settles it (the `runaway` block of its `magic-rules.json`). Each later turn, before spells are renewed, the spell drains its original casting cost **plus 1** (pushed mana counts in the cost): mana first, and once the pool is empty the caster falls unconscious and the rest comes off Body; at 0 Body the caster dies. The conscious caster may spend their action on an **Anchor Roll**, 3d6 against Wisdom less the same injury and off-balance penalties a casting takes. Another mage may spend theirs to help: they pay **twice** the original cost in mana whatever happens and roll 2d10 against Intelligence, and if that fails they are caught in a Runaway of the same spell. A caster feeds one Runaway at a time. Once anchored, a continuing spell is the anchorer's to renew or let lapse; any other spell simply ends. The spell's growth while it ran is the Game Master's to describe, and the board adds **+2** per turn it ran to the rolled effect of the anchorer's next cast of that spell, when it is not a continuing one. The board does not make the Anchor Roll harder each round; the Game Master may.

Those who survive often carry marks: white streaks in the hair, strange scars, a haunted look. See the public [Runaway](/tarmar/reference/public-rules/magic/runaway/) page.
