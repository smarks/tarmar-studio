# Counterspell

A caster may try to counter an enemy's spell by committing mana in a contest of will. The defender commits mana and both make opposed Intelligence checks; the more mana committed, the better the defender's chance.

If the caster wins, the spell goes through. If the defender wins, the spell is countered, and the caster may try again next turn, escalating the duel. Only the caster may escalate; the defender gets one chance to stop each casting.

On the battle board this default canon settles it (the `counterspell` block of its `magic-rules.json`). Any mage may spend its action to counter an enemy mage, committing **1 to 5** mana; the counter names the caster, not a spell, and meets whatever that caster casts this turn. If the caster casts nothing, the committed mana is spent all the same. When the spell is cast, before its casting roll, both sides roll **2d10 plus Intelligence**: the caster adds **+1 for every 2** mana the casting cost (pushed mana included), the defender +1 for every 2 mana committed, and each side takes its own injury penalty (the caster its off-balance penalty too). The caster must beat every defender's total; a **tie goes to the defender**. A countered spell makes no casting roll and its mana stays spent. The caster escalates by casting the same spell at the same target on a later turn, paying again; a defender who wants to oppose it again counters again, and one who does not lets it through. Choosing anything else ends the duel. Only a person counters or escalates; computer-run figures do neither.

These duels can drain both sides quickly, and a failed Control Roll during escalation can trigger [Runaway](/tarmar/reference/dm-rules/magic-mechanics/runaway/). See the public [Counterspell](/tarmar/reference/public-rules/magic/counterspell/) page.
