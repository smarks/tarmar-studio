# Spell Descriptions

Every spell in the [Schools of Magic](../schools-of-magic) list, described, with
the attribute that matters most when you cast it.

**Reading the tables:** *Lvl* is the spell's tier within its school, and per
[Casting Spells](../casting-spells) it's also the **mana cost** — a level 4
spell costs 4 mana to cast. *Affinity* is whichever of INT or WIS the
[Casting Roll](../casting-spells) uses for that spell (3d6 ≤ your attribute);
*Why* is a one-line reason, not a house rule — your GM has final say on any
spell not listed here.

**The general split:** INT governs spells that require precise technical
control — shaping raw force, working a mechanism, crafting a false
sense-impression exactly. WIS governs spells that call on judgment,
insight, or endurance — reading a mind, reading the weather, sensing what a
wound or a threat needs. Most schools lean one way; a few split spell by
spell.

## Elemental

Common school. Every spell below can be cast in any of the five elemental
flavors — **water, fire, lightning, earth, wind** — chosen when you learn
it; a caster who wants more than one flavor of the same spell learns it
again. All Elemental spells are **INT**: shaping a bolt of flame or a wall
of stone into an exact form is technique, not insight.

| Spell | Lvl | Affinity | Why | Description |
| ----- | --- | -------- | --- | ------------ |
| Missile | 1 | INT | Precise single-target force needs technical control. | A small, fast dart of your chosen element, flung at one target. The bread-and-butter attack spell — cheap, quick to cast, and (per [Casting Spells](../casting-spells)) needs a hit roll on top of the casting roll, since it has to land as well as form. |
| Ball | 2 | INT | Same technique, shaped to spread instead of pierce. | A globe of your element that expands to strike everyone in a small area, not just one target. Costs more to hold its shape as it grows — friendly fire is a real risk in a tight formation. |
| Bolt | 3 | INT | A stronger, single-line application of the same control. | A punishing line of your element, faster and harder-hitting than a Missile, still aimed at one target or in a line through several. The step up in level buys raw power, not new tricks. |
| Shield (Elemental) | 4 | INT | Holding a precise, constantly-adjusting ward around yourself. | A personal ward that dampens or deflects incoming attacks of the caster's chosen element. It has to track and cancel force in real time, which is why it costs more than the offensive spells beneath it. Distinct from Protection's **Shield**, below — this one only stops its own element. |
| Barrier | 5 | INT | Shaping a standing, lasting structure out of raw element. | A wall or dome of the chosen element, large enough to block movement and line of sight, or to box enemies in. Holds its shape without the caster's continued attention once it's up, though it still needs renewal to persist past the turn it's cast. |
| Elemental | 6 | INT | The school's capstone: total command of the medium. | The full expression of the school — a lasting, powerful manifestation of the caster's element, closer to summoning a localized force of nature than throwing a spell. GMs should treat its exact effect as a set-piece, not a fixed stat block. |

## Passage

Common school. Detection, access, and movement magic — mostly technical
alteration of physical reality, which is why it stays **INT** almost
straight down the list.

| Spell | Lvl | Affinity | Why | Description |
| ----- | --- | -------- | --- | ------------ |
| Detect Magic | 1 | INT | Reading a trace of worked power is perception, not judgment. | Reveals whether magic has touched an object, place, or person nearby — not what it does, just that it's there. Also appears in Protection's spell list at the same level; it's one spell, learnable from either track. |
| Light | 1 | INT | A simple, exact technical effect. | Conjures a small, steady light source at a point the caster designates — no heat, no fuel, no flicker. The first spell most casters ever learn. |
| Knock | 2 | INT | Forcing a mechanism is a technical problem. | Pops a mundane lock or latch without a key, working the mechanism from a distance rather than breaking it. Wards and magical locks resist it; ordinary ones rarely do. |
| Hold | 2 | INT | Precisely pinning an object or door in place. | Fixes a door, object, or small structure so it cannot be opened, moved, or forced by ordinary means until the spell lapses or is countered. |
| Blur | 2 | INT | Crafting an exact false sense-impression. | Smears the caster's outline just enough to spoil an attacker's aim — treat it as the caster's own dodge trick, not true invisibility. Referenced again, thematically, under Control's Minor Illusion, which produces a similar look through a different school. |
| Transparency | 3 | INT | A harder, more precise version of the same trick. | Renders the target (self or another) invisible or see-through for the duration — a full step up from Blur's mere distortion. Control's **Illusion** spell can mimic the effect on someone else's target, at a higher level and less reliably. |
| Detect Invisibility | 3 | INT | Perception again — seeing through a technique, not judging intent. | Lets the caster see anything hidden by invisibility or similar concealment magic for the duration, including the caster's own Transparency countered against them. |
| Lock | 3 | INT | The offensive twin of Knock: precisely jamming a mechanism. | Seals a mundane lock, latch, or door shut against anyone but the caster — the reverse of Knock, and just as easily countered by a rival caster's Knock. |
| Breathing | 4 | INT | Sustaining an exact physical condition around the body. | Lets the target breathe normally underwater, in smoke, or in thin air for the duration — a precise, sustained alteration of the air immediately around them rather than a change to their body. |
| Flying | 5 | INT | Continuous, exacting control over the target's own momentum. | Grants the target true, controlled flight for the duration. High-cost and high-value: it turns the vertical axis and any water or chasm into a non-obstacle, which is exactly why it sits near the top of the school. |
| Pass Wall | 6 | INT | Precisely unmaking a solid barrier for a moment. | Opens a temporary passage straight through a wall or similar solid barrier, closing again once the spell ends (with anyone still inside when it closes at the GM's mercy). |
| Shape Change | 7 | INT | The school's capstone: total technical control of one's own form. | Reshapes the caster's own body into another form — animal, or something stranger — for the duration. The highest-level Passage spell for a reason: getting every detail of an unfamiliar body right, and getting back, is exacting work. |

## Protection

Common school. Warding and defensive magic — instinct and judgment about
what's coming, which puts most of it on the **WIS** side of the split.

| Spell | Lvl | Affinity | Why | Description |
| ----- | --- | -------- | --- | ------------ |
| Counter | 1–5 | WIS | Reading an opposing caster's intent and will, not shaping force. | Invests mana to oppose an enemy spell as it's cast — this is the spell version of the [Counterspell](../counterspell) duel: commit mana, roll opposed against the attacker, and win or lose the contest. Its five tiers are the five levels of investment a caster can bring to that duel, not five different spells. |
| Shield (Protection) | 1 | WIS | A defensive instinct, not a shaped effect. | A general ward that improves the target's chances against an incoming attack (mundane or magical) rather than blocking one element specifically. Where Elemental's **Shield** is a precise dampening field, this one is closer to a heightened sense of danger made physical. |
| Detect Magic | 1 | INT | Reading a trace of worked power is perception, not judgment. | See Passage, above — the same spell, taught on this track as well. |
| Shield Wall | 2 | WIS | Extending protective instinct to a group. | Extends a Shield-like ward across a line of allies rather than a single target — a formation spell, cast by whoever is standing where the enemy will hit hardest. |
| Knowledge | 2 | WIS | Insight and judgment about what's known, not a technical readout. | Surfaces relevant facts the caster (or a target) doesn't consciously know, at the GM's discretion — closer to a flash of applied wisdom than a spell-as-search-engine. |
| Minor Protection from Element | 3 | WIS | Judging and blunting a specific danger. | Grants meaningful resistance to one element (fire, cold, and so on) for the duration — the practical answer to Elemental's offensive spells. |
| Major Protection from Element | 4 | WIS | The stronger form of the same protective judgment. | As Minor Protection, but the resistance is much harder to overcome — the answer to a caster who's learned Barrier or Elemental in that same element. |
| Encase | 5 | WIS | A defensive judgment call taken to its extreme: total isolation. | Seals the target (willing or not) inside a nearly impenetrable shell — the strongest single-target defense in the school, and a favorite for containing something dangerous rather than protecting an ally. |

## Mending

Common school. Healing and repair — sensing what a body or an object needs
to be whole, which is **WIS**'s domain even though the mundane [Medic
skill](/tarmar/reference/public-rules/characters/prior-experience/skills-glossary/)
that does similar work is INT-governed. The skill is trained clinical
technique; the spell is closer to a felt sense of what's wrong.

| Spell | Lvl | Affinity | Why | Description |
| ----- | --- | -------- | --- | ------------ |
| Heal | 1 | WIS | Sensing and closing a wound is insight, not force. | Restores Fatigue (and, on a strong casting roll, some Body) to the target — the school's foundation spell and the most commonly known spell in Tarmar for exactly that reason. |
| Cure | 2 | WIS | Judging what's poisoning or afflicting the body. | Removes poison, disease, or a similar ongoing affliction from the target, rather than restoring points directly the way Heal does. |
| Web/Joining | 3 | WIS | Sensing where two things belong together. | Binds a target in place with sticky, rope-like strands (Web), or fuses two broken pieces of an object or wound edge back together (Joining) — two applications of the same "make things adhere" principle, one hostile, one restorative. |
| Breaking | 3 | WIS | The inverse judgment: sensing a structure's weak point. | Weakens or shatters an object's structural integrity at a chosen point — Web/Joining's opposite number, aimed at things rather than people. |
| Shaping | 4 | WIS | The school's capstone: full sculptural insight into material. | Reshapes an existing quantity of material (stone, wood, metal, flesh) into a new form the caster has in mind, gradually rather than instantly — closer to expert sculpture than teleportation-of-matter. |

## Weather

Less common school (71–100%). Reading and, eventually, bending the sky —
**WIS** all the way through, matching the mundane [Weather/Path
skill](/tarmar/reference/public-rules/characters/prior-experience/skills-glossary/)'s
own WIS governance.

| Spell | Lvl | Affinity | Why | Description |
| ----- | --- | -------- | --- | ------------ |
| Detect Weather | 1 | WIS | Reading the sky is awareness and judgment. | Tells the caster what weather is coming over the next short while — the entry point to the school and a staple for anyone who sails or campaigns by it. |
| Wind | 2 | WIS | Calling weather is an extension of reading it. | Raises or redirects a local wind — enough to fill a sail, scatter smoke, or spoil an archer's aim. |
| Wind Counter | 2 | WIS | Judging and canceling an opposing pattern. | Cancels or calms an existing wind effect, mundane or magical — Wind's opposite number. |
| Rain | 3 | WIS | A stronger application of the same weather-sense. | Calls rain over an area — useful for dousing fire, ruining a pursuer's tracks, or simply making everyone miserable. |
| Rain Counter | 3 | WIS | Judging and clearing an opposing pattern. | Clears rain, mundane or conjured, from the area — Rain's opposite number. |
| Storm | 4 | WIS | The strongest weather-calling the school offers. | Summons a genuine storm — heavy rain or snow, high wind, and reduced visibility over a real area, not just around the caster. |
| Storm Counter | 4 | WIS | The strongest weather-canceling judgment the school offers. | Breaks an existing storm, mundane or conjured — Storm's opposite number and the top of the school. |

## Control

Less common school. Splits down the middle: spells that work on a mind
(insight, so **WIS**) versus spells that work through a crafted false
sense-impression or raw compulsion (technique, so **INT**).

| Spell | Lvl | Affinity | Why | Description |
| ----- | --- | -------- | --- | ------------ |
| Persuade Minor | 1 | WIS | Reading a mind well enough to nudge it. | Makes a suggestion feel more reasonable to the target than it otherwise would — a magical thumb on the scale of an ordinary conversation, not mind control. |
| Calm | 1 | WIS | Judging and settling a target's emotional state. | Soothes a frightened, enraged, or panicking target toward a level head. Works on crowds as easily as individuals, which makes it a favorite for riot control. |
| Enrage | 1 | WIS | The same insight, aimed the other direction. | Calm's opposite number: pushes a target toward anger or aggression, useful for turning a calm negotiation hostile or a wavering line into a charge. |
| Minor Illusion | 2 | INT | Crafting a false sense-impression is exacting technique. | Produces a small false sight, sound, or sensation — a flicker, a whisper, a shape at the edge of vision. Covers effects in the spirit of Passage's **Blur**, though built through a different school and less durable than a dedicated Passage spell. |
| Fatigue | 2 | INT | A direct, technical drain, closer to a weapon than a mind-effect. | Saps the target's Fatigue points directly, the spell-equivalent of exhausting someone through pure magical force rather than a blow. |
| Wound | 3 | INT | A direct, technical drain reaching further than Fatigue. | As Fatigue, but hits harder and can reach Body as well — the school's answer to a straightforward damage spell. |
| Effect Mental State/Senses | 3 | WIS | Reading a mind precisely enough to override one of its senses or states. | A family of single-effect spells at this level: **Sleep**, **Confusion**, **Blind**, **Deafen**, **Laugh** — each imposes exactly what it says on the target's mind or senses for the duration. The caster learns them individually; treat each as level 3. |
| Persuade Major | 4 | WIS | The stronger form of the same mind-reading judgment. | As Persuade Minor, but the suggestion can run against the target's own interests and still land, given a strong enough casting roll. |
| Illusion | 4 | INT | A larger, more convincing crafted sense-impression. | Produces a more complete and durable false sight or sound than Minor Illusion — a real phantom rather than a flicker. Can approximate Passage's **Transparency** on an unwilling target, at the cost of a higher level and less certainty. |
| Major Illusion | 5 | INT | The strongest single-sense illusion the school offers. | A large, detailed, and durable illusion, potentially fooling more than one sense at once and holding up to real scrutiny far longer than Illusion. |
| Area Effect | 5 | INT | Extending precise sensory craft across a whole area. | Not a single effect but a modifier: lets the caster spread another Control illusion or sense-effect across an area rather than a single target — usually paired with Illusion or Major Illusion. |
| Effect Senses | 5 | INT | Directly overriding a target's senses is technical, not persuasive. | Blocks, dulls, or falsifies one of the target's senses outright (beyond the specific Blind/Deafen pair at level 3) — a broader, higher-level sensory override. |
| Force | 6 | INT | Raw compulsion of a body against its will is pure technique. | The school's capstone: a family of effects — **Paralyze**, **Move**, **Breath**, **Run**, **Sleep**, and others at the GM's discretion — that physically compel the target's body rather than persuading their mind. Each still costs a level-6 casting; a caster typically knows it for one or two named effects, not the whole family at once. |

## Open questions for the author

- **Element choice at character creation vs. per-spell:** whether a caster
  fixes one elemental flavor for their whole Elemental track or picks it
  spell-by-spell is left to the GM until ruled.
- **"Force …, etc." (Control 6):** the source list leaves this open-ended;
  treat Paralyze/Move/Breath/Run/Sleep as the known set and let the GM add
  more at the same level.
