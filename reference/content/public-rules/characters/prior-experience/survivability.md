# Survivability

Each year in a track requires a survival roll. Roll 3d6 + your best attribute modifier against your track's target. A natural 18 always survives; a natural 3 always dies.

| Track           | Survival (3d6) | XP/Year | Money/Year                |
| --------------- | -------------- | ------- | ------------------------- |
| Merchant        | 4+             | 750     | 100 x highest skill level |
| Campaigner      | 9+             | 1,200   | 200 x highest skill level |
| Laborer         | 2+             | 500     | 25 x highest skill level  |
| Magic           | 9+             | 900     | 75 x highest skill level  |
| Underworld      | 8+             | 1,000   | 125 x highest skill level |
| Civil Service   | 4+             | 750     | 75 x highest skill level  |
| Craft           | 3+             | 600     | 75 x craft skill level    |
| Hunter/Gatherer | 6+             | 800     | 25 x highest skill level  |

The targets are the same numbers the roll used when it was 2d6. Three dice
cluster around 10 or 11 instead of 7, so the same target is markedly kinder --
that is the point. Prior experience should be survivable enough to be worth
playing out.

## Your best attribute modifier

"Best" means exactly that: take the modifier of your **single highest**
attribute of the six and add it. You never add more than one attribute's
modifier, and you never total them up.

A penalty therefore reaches the roll only when it is the best you have — a
character whose every attribute is 7 or below rolls at a minus, while one weak
attribute alongside an average one changes nothing. That is deliberate: prior
experience asks whether you had *any* quality to trade on, not whether you were
well-rounded.

The modifier is figured fresh **every year**, from the attributes you have that
year:

- A **+1 STR / +1 DEX / +1 CON** result raises the attribute at once, so it
  counts from the following year's roll onward.
- **Aging** penalties (see [Aging](../aging)) come off as soon as you reach the
  age that carries them, so the year after your birthday is rolled at the
  weaker score.

Rolling four years one at a time and rolling a four-year run therefore give
exactly the same modifiers.

## Money

Money is figured separately for each year, using the relevant skill level
(highest skill overall, or Maker Skill for Craft) as of that point in the
simulation -- points rolled in an earlier year count right away. Everyone earns
at least the track's base rate: with no skill in the relevant area, the level
used for pay is floored at 1 (never 0), so even a skill-less character earns the
multiplier shown above, not zero.
