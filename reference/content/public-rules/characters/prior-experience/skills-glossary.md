# Skills Glossary

Every skill the prior-experience generator can grant, the dice roll that grants
it, and **what it does at the table**. Use this with [Skill Tracks](../skill-tracks)
to roll up a character's past by hand — exactly the way the app does it.

## What a skill does

A skill makes you better at one kind of task. When you attempt something a skill
covers:

> **Skill check:** roll **2d6 + the governing attribute's modifier + your skill
> level**, against a difficulty the GM sets. A natural 12 always succeeds; a
> natural 2 always fails. Untrained, you roll with the attribute modifier alone.

Skill level adds **+1 per level**, so a skill contributes:

| Level | I | II | III | IV | V | VI |
| ----- | - | -- | --- | -- | - | -- |
| Bonus | +1 | +2 | +3 | +4 | +5 | +6 |

**There is no cap** — on the level or on the bonus. The table simply stops
where the page runs out of room; level VII is +7 and so on.

Levels are bought with skill points, and every skill charges the same:
**level 1 costs 1 point, level 2 costs 2 more, level 3 costs 3 more**, and so
on. Reaching level N therefore costs **N(N+1)/2** points in total:

| Level | I | II | III | IV | V | VI |
| ----- | - | -- | --- | -- | - | -- |
| Cumulative cost | 1 | 3 | 6 | 10 | 15 | 21 |

**Exception:** the **Arcane** skill (True Magic) costs **double** — level 1
costs 2 points, level N costs 2×N more, so reaching level N costs **N(N+1)**
points in total.

(See [Prior Experience](../../prior-experience) for where the points come
from.) A level V skill (+5) is worth as much as a peak attribute, and climbing
past it costs steeply but is never barred — so specializing matters. The
**Governs** column below names the attribute whose modifier the skill rides on;
the GM may substitute a better-fit attribute for an unusual use.

## Naming and renaming a skill

A skill's name says which rule it falls under, and renaming one never moves its
points to another skill. You make two choices by name: the craft of a **Maker
Skill** (once), and a **Village** origin's Street Smarts or Survival. Focusing
a **Knowledge** on a field is the GM's to allow. A **Weapon** skill is tied to its weapon the same
way — *Weapon (Broadsword)* — see [Weapon Classes](#weapon-classes). Moving
points from one skill to another is the GM's call alone.

## How a skill is granted

Each **year** in a track, **roll 1d20**; the result (1–20) is the position in
your track's list — that's the skill you gain. Roll a skill you already have and
the point goes toward its next level. Buy as many years as you like, one at a
time or in a run. See [Skill Tracks](../skill-tracks).

The **Rolled on** column lists every track and the d20 value(s) that grant the
skill. Read *Merchant 3* as "on the Merchant track, a roll of 3 grants it";
*Magic 14, 15* means a 14 or 15 on the Magic track. Track names are spelled out
in full.

## Weapon Classes

When you gain the **Weapon** skill, tie it to one specific weapon. You add your
**full** skill level to attacks with that exact weapon, and **half your level
(rounded down)** to attacks with any other weapon of the **same class**. A
weapon of a different class gets no bonus from that skill.

*Example:* Weapon (Broadsword) at Level IV gives **+4** with a broadsword and
**+2** with any other Striking weapon — a mace, a war ax, a saber.

**Unusual weapons are the exception:** they are too unlike one another to share
training, so each requires its **own** Weapon skill. A skill tied to an Unusual
weapon — quarterstaff, net, cestus, whip, lasso, boomerang, nunchaku, bola,
torch — gives its full level with that exact weapon and transfers **nothing**
in either direction, even with a weapon that shares its class below.

Weapons are grouped by **how their attack defeats armour**, not by shape, and
the classes are exactly the ones the [attack-roll Target Number
matrix](../../../combat/action-options/attack-rolls) uses — one source of
truth. Every catalog weapon carries its class in the `weapon_class` field:

| Class | Includes |
| ----- | -------- |
| Piercing | dagger, rapier |
| Striking | saber, shortsword, broadsword, bastard sword (1 hand), club, hatchet, hammer, mace, small ax, war ax, morningstar, quarterstaff, cestus, nunchaku, torch |
| Thrusting | javelin, spear, trident |
| Heavy Striking | bastard sword (2 hands), 2-handed sword, great sword, great hammer, battle axe |
| Heavy Thrusting | halberd, pike ax, pike, cavalry lance |
| Missile — Bows | sling, small bow, horse bow, longbow, thrown rock, boomerang |
| Missile — Crossbows | light crossbow, heavy crossbow |
| Flexible / Snare | whip, net, lasso, bola |

(Ammunition is not a weapon and has no class.)

**Archery** is, in effect, the Weapon skill for shooting — use it for ranged
attacks across both Missile classes.

## Manual roll-up in brief

1. Pick a [track](../skill-tracks). Start at age 16, level 1.
2. For each year: roll **1d20** → take that position's skill; make the
   **3d6 + best modifier** [survival roll](../survivability) (natural 18
   lives, natural 3 dies); add the track's XP and money; age 1 year.
3. Repeat for as many years as you want. From age 36 on, apply
   [aging](../aging) penalties as each four-year step is reached.
4. Convert total XP to skill points using the table in
   [Prior Experience](../../prior-experience), then spend them.
