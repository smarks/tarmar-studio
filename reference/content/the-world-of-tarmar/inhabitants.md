# Inhabitants

*Draft — fleshed out from notes; refine the canon as you like.*

The world of Tarmar is populated by a plethora of humanoids and not-quite-humanoids: classic monsters like the manticore and the goblin, a wide range of undead, and other mythological beings all the way out to the downright ethereal. And, of course, there are dragons and trolls.

But there are other beings the standard traveler may never have heard of — the venomous spider-bat, the umber hulk, the Dark Men — and, well, we will leave a more exhaustive list to your imagination and to the fruits of your own exploration.

Overwhelmingly, humans are the most common sentient race, but they are far from alone. There are giants and dwarves; common elves and dark elves. There are also, some claim, fairies — as well as the elusive little people. There are even the Dragonborn, whose origins are mostly a mystery to anyone who is not one.

And then there are the things that turn up where no one expects them: giant sea creatures, carnivorous plants, and sentient beings hiding in plain sight.

Many humans understand that they are not alone in the universe — that Stephen's World may be just one of the countless points of light scattered across the night sky. But not everybody believes it, and among those who do, the specifics vary wildly.

## The Sentient Races

### Humans

Humans are, well, standard humans. Common, adaptable, and found nearly everywhere — which is exactly why the rest of the world tends to measure itself against them.

### Dwarves

Dwarves are fairly standard too. They come from the earth, and yes, there are female dwarves — though to a non-dwarf they nearly all look like hairy little trolls. Even the well-to-do ones look like *well-groomed* hairy little trolls.

They make their homes in the far northern reaches but can be found wandering throughout the world; the farther south you travel, the less likely you are to meet one. They would rather stay in their halls and make things — and dwarf-made equipment is reckoned, and genuinely is, superior to nearly anything else you can buy.

### Elves

The common elves are long-lived, keen-eyed, and more at home in forest and field than in any city. They keep to their own lands and their own counsel, and regard the affairs of shorter-lived folk with patient detachment.

### Dark Elves

Dark elves hail from the Kanu Imperium, an ancient people who hearken back to the very beginning of recorded time. Dark of skin and hair and typically of slighter build, they are otherwise humanoid — so much so that some insist they are simply another flavor of human, designated "elves" only because they are uncommon and come from the Kanu Imperium.

### Dragonborn

Scaled, proud, and rare, the Dragonborn walk among the other peoples while keeping their own ways close. Where they came from — and what, exactly, binds them to the dragons whose name they carry — is a mystery to nearly everyone who is not one. The Dragonborn, for their part, are in no hurry to explain.

### Giants

Giants are exactly what the name promises: enormous, powerful, and not always friendly. Some keep to remote mountains and frozen wastes; others have learned to live, warily, at the margins of smaller folk's lands.

### The Little People

Fairies and the elusive "little people" sit at the very edge of what most travelers will ever confirm with their own eyes. Glimpsed more often than met, they are the subject of a thousand stories and very few reliable accounts. Whether they are one people or many — or real at all — depends entirely on whom you ask.

## Beasts, Monsters, and Stranger Things

For every sentient race there are a dozen things that simply want to eat you — and a few that want something harder to name.

- **Dragons** — vast, ancient, and unforgettable. (See [The Dragons](/tarmar/reference/the-world-of-tarmar/dragons/).)
- **Trolls** — big, strong, and infamously hard to put down for good.
- **Goblins** — small, numerous, and far more dangerous in a crowd than alone.
- **Manticores** — winged horrors with a cunning to match their hunger.
- **The undead** — from shambling and mindless to ancient and patient, the dead in Tarmar do not always stay dead.
- **The venomous spider-bat** — exactly as unpleasant as it sounds, and quicker than you would like.
- **Umber hulks** — burrowing brutes whose gaze is said to scramble the mind of anyone who meets it.
- **The Dark Men** — rarely seen, never explained, and best not followed.
- **Giant sea creatures** — the deep water keeps its own monsters, and sailors keep their own prayers.
- **Carnivorous plants** — not every danger in Tarmar can chase you. Some of them simply wait.

And beyond these? Well — we will leave the rest to your imagination, and to whatever you find out there.
